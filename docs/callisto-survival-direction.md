# Callisto survival direction

**Status: proposed, unimplemented.** This is a small playable experiment to discuss and test, not approval for a rewrite. Preserve the current cruise and colony trial until the surface slice proves worthwhile.

## The experience

Manage one fragile outpost and send crews beyond it. The Frostpunk influence is pressure from scarce supplies, competing jobs, and expeditions that can improve tomorrow by weakening the base today. There is no city layout, population simulation, law tree, or technology tree in this episode.

## First episode

Six operational watches, one habitat, two crew teams, and the existing power unit, cooling loop, and recycler. Begin with finite support supplies, spares, and fabrication feedstock. Equipment condition is visible in this first test. Reuse the engineering model's capacity limits, wear, repair downtime, project power allocation, and reserve consumption rather than building a parallel survival engine.

Each watch, assign the teams to base work or a surface trip, choose operating priorities, and run the plan. An away team cannot inspect, repair, fabricate, or commission at the base that watch. Supply demand represents support for the whole expedition, including crews outside the habitat.

Only two destinations exist. Each can be completed once; proposed quantities below are tuning values, not physical measurements.

| Destination | Disclosed commitment | Return |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Our equipment pallet | One team away for one watch; spend one support supply on field operations | Recover two support supplies and one spare |
| Ice survey site | One team away for one watch; spend one support supply on field operations | Confirm a candidate water-ice site and return a sample; complete the episode's survey objective |

Show departure cost, return watch, and known reward before dispatch. Returns occur at the end of the watch, before checking the remaining supply buffer; returned materials become usable next watch. Returning supplies cannot cover an unmet demand that already occurred. The survey does not instantly create drinking water or introduce a mining subsystem.

One emergency operating choice is enough: override protection for this watch to deliver more power. Preview the resulting supply use, stalled/completed jobs, and added wear. Each power or cooling unit pushed past its modeled limit gains one wear, following the existing engineering rules. The extra output may finish habitat work now while reducing later capacity.

## Outcome

**Win:** by the end of watch six, complete both habitat commissioning steps, meet recycling demand in that watch, retain at least one support supply, and return the ice survey team. This means surviving the establishment episode with a future water-source lead; it does not establish long-term self-sufficiency.

**Fail:** fail to cover life-support demand during a watch, have no support supplies after its expedition returns, or reach watch six without meeting the win conditions. Explain the failure through the recorded supply shortfall or unfinished work. Do not add random death rolls.

## Assumptions and boundaries

The pallet is ours, staged during landing. Earlier robotic cargo delivery would be new fiction and needs an explicit decision before adoption. There are no native inhabitants or discovered ruins. Travel distances and watch duration need definition before assigning real calendar times. Support supplies and machinery capacities remain disclosed gameplay abstractions, not a validated physiological or thermodynamic simulation. The 24-month cruise calendar is still unresolved.

Generate any starting variation from the voyage seed, then keep outcomes deterministic for identical choices. Begin with disclosed equipment condition and destination outcomes; test whether the resource decisions work before introducing incomplete information. Later cruise handoff can map preserved Cargo to starting supplies, repair materials, and science capabilities, but that mapping is outside this first experiment.

## Acceptance

Verify two useful viable approaches in the same starting case: repair early and preserve supplies while delaying the pallet trip; or retrieve the pallet early, accept temporary supply/operating costs, and repair or commission afterward. Neither should require servicing every unit on a compulsory rotation. Players should be able to explain why sending a team away was worth losing its base assignment, and trace a later shortage to an earlier choice. Tune this episode before adding destinations, crises, or settlement growth.
